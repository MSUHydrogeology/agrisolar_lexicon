Appendix A — Index — Alphabetical

Every defined or coined term in the lexicon, ordered A–Z. This is the alphabetical view of the Terminology chapter (§7), which holds the same set grouped by subject — use this one when you know the word, that one when you want the neighborhood it belongs to.

Each entry gives a short gloss and two links: §7 for the definition, and, where a chapter develops the term, the section that does so. The built-in site search (top of every page) complements both — search finds a word anywhere in the prose, while this list is the curated set of terms the document actually defines.

Zone codes A0–A5 are grouped under A; sharing positions S1–S5 under S.

A

A0 — the zero-land case — arrays on buildings, which take no ground at all: no siting latitude, no ground layer, and no sharing–sparing position; the transect’s origin rather than a point on it — §7 · 1.2 The Zones

A1 — Farmstead — <100 kW, behind-the-meter, single owner-operator, existing farm labor — §7 · 1.2 The Zones

A2 — Field — 100 kW–1 MW, behind-meter to distribution, landowner with possibly one lease counterparty — §7 · 1.2 The Zones

A3 — Parcel — 1–10 MW, distribution / community solar, multi-party, purpose-built ground management — §7 · 1.2 The Zones

A4 — Landscape — 10–50 MW, developer-led institutional capital, contract-enterprise management — §7 · 1.2 The Zones

A5 — Regional — 50 MW+, transmission, utility/IPP, contract enterprise at ranch scale — §7 · 1.2 The Zones

Agricultural production — design pressure: keep working land working — §7 · 3.1 The pressures

Agrisolar — agroenergy specific to photovoltaics; the lexicon’s working umbrella (after NCAT’s AgriSolar Clearinghouse usage), broadened to be neutral across the sharing–sparing axis — §7

Agrivoltaics — crop or livestock production within the array footprint (S1–S2); cf. agrophotovoltaics, photovoltaic agriculture — §7

Agroenergy — energy infrastructure designed to interact intentionally with agricultural or ecological land function; umbrella term — §7

Archetype — a way the design pressures have been resolved at a particular scale zone and sharing position — §7 · 5. Archetypes

B

Bare conversion — ordinary ground converted with the farm operation ended: the same ground fact as S3 without the restorative one. The non-restorative baseline the axis is read against, not a position on it — §7 · 2.1 The positions

Benefit delivery — how a function’s benefit reaches whoever receives it: by proximity, by channel, or by preservation; a restorative claim is not checkable until its delivery is named — §7 · 4.6 Where each function applies

Biodiversity & habitat function — restorative function: habitat built from the ground layer up, with mowing and grazing timing as decisive as the seed mix; reachable across S2–S5 — §7 · 4.3 Biodiversity & habitat function

C

Build cost — design pressure: minimize installed cost per watt — §7 · 3.1 The pressures

Carrying capacity — season-long forage the land sustains over time; a grazing design variable — §7 · 1.2 Worked example: grazing

Coexistence (S3) — see S3 — Coexistence; the center of the sharing–sparing axis, neither sharing nor sparing — §7 · 2.1 The positions

Co-benefit — the field’s usual word for a restorative function; recorded but not adopted, because its grammar demotes what it names to something arriving alongside the real objective — §7

Co-location — panels and a land use share a parcel without functional interdependence; cf. integration — §7

Community & economic function — the socioeconomic restorative function: economic prosperity, livability, and health & equity built for the host community; the SCALE Communities domain read as an outcome, distinct from the social-license pressure and community-benefit lever — §7 · 4.5 Community & economic function

Community Benefit — instruments returning value to the host community (CBA/HCA, PILOT, good-neighbor payments, community-solar subscription, co-ownership, decommissioning bond); distinct from acceptance — §7

Contract grazing — grazing delivered as a contracted vegetation-management service by a party who does not live on site — §7 · 1.2 Worked example: grazing

D

Decommissioning / Repowering / Reversibility — end-of-life questions: removing and restoring, replacing modules, and how completely the prior land use can return — §7

End-of-life responsibility — a design pressure: minimize the residual footprint and preserve the option to return the ground to its prior use — §7 · 3.1 The pressures

Delivery by preservation — the mode in which nothing moves and the benefit is ground nearby that stayed as it was; proximate, because development pressure is local, but counterfactual rather than observed — §7 · 4.6 Where each function applies

Design import — the characteristic regional failure: carrying a resolution from the region where it was demonstrated into one whose factors differ; grid and market demands is the usual vehicle — §7 · 6.3 What follows

Design levers — the degrees of freedom — structural, land-and-ecology, institutional — through which pressures get resolved — §7 · 3.3 Design levers

Design pressures — the competing forces every site must resolve; unlike axes they have direction, conflict, and advocates — §7 · 3. Design Pressures

Diffuse fraction — the share of irradiance arriving as diffuse rather than direct-beam light; higher under cloud, non-directional, and most of what a ground layer lives on — §7 · 6.1 Six factors

Dual Use — production-based criterion: active agricultural production in the footprint across the project life (AFT’s definition); S1–S2 — §7

E

Ecovoltaics — solar development incorporating ecological understanding into array design and management, co-prioritizing ecosystem function with generation; its ecological ground layer is a restorative function rather than an axis position, most often built at S3 — §7

F

Feasible wedge — the feasible region across scale and sharing: all five sharing positions stay open through A3, narrowing toward the S2–S3 middle at both extremes — §7 · 2.2 Which positions scale allows

Footprint — land physically occupied by racking, panels, and access infrastructure; paired with influence zone — §7

G

Energy yield — design pressure: maximize energy per unit land — §7 · 3.1 The pressures

Ground Coverage Ratio (GCR) — ratio of module area to ground area; the array’s density; the number behind the row-geometry ↔︎ ground-layer coupling — §7

Ground Layer — whatever occupies the surface beneath and between panels; primary carrier of restorative function; syn. understory, ground cover — §7

H

Hydrologic function — restorative function: infiltration and recharge raised, runoff slowed and filtered, soil moisture buffered — and at scale, consumptive use retired from a basin’s budget — §7 · 4.1 Hydrologic function

I

Influence Zone — the larger, typically asymmetric area whose hydrology, microclimate, soil, or wildlife movement is measurably affected — §7

Integration — functional interdependence — panel height set by livestock clearance, spacing by forage light, tracking by photosynthetic timing — §7

L

Land Equivalent Ratio (LER) — standard dual-use productivity metric; handles the non-production ground layers of S3–S5 poorly — §7

Land sharing — production continues on the ground the array occupies; the sharing wing of the axis (S1–S2). Borrowed from conservation ecology — §7 · 2. The Sharing–Sparing Axis

Land sparing — ground comes out of production so other ground can be left alone; the sparing wing of the axis (S4–S5). Borrowed from conservation ecology — §7 · 2. The Sharing–Sparing Axis

M

Management-led — restoration mode: placement is a given; ground cover, grazing regime, and operational schedule carry everything — §7 · 1.3 Siting latitude and the restoration mode shift

Marginal / Previously Disturbed Land — the low-value ground that idled-land sparing (S4) presupposes: low-yielding cropland, reclaimed mine land, landfills, brownfields (“brightfields”) — §7

Microclimate & thermal–water buffering function — restorative function: the array’s reshaping of the near-surface energy balance; its sign flips with climate and crop — §7 · 4.4 Microclimate & thermal–water buffering function

Module Technology — the panel as a lever: monofacial/bifacial, vertical bifacial, semitransparent, spectrally selective — §7

Multi-use solar — the field’s competing umbrella (Merheb et al. 2025), covering agrivoltaics, rangevoltaics, and ecovoltaics; narrower than agrisolar as used here, which also covers S3–S5 — §7

O

Grid and market demands — design pressure: satisfy the criteria of whoever buys the power, attributes, or tax position — §7 · 3.1 The pressures

Operations and maintenance — design pressure: minimize management burden across a 25–40 year asset life — §7 · 3.1 The pressures

Overhead and interspace systems — the engineering standards’ hardware typology (DIN SPEC 91434; NREL/InSPIRE): modules above the working surface, or low with the crop between rows; classifies structure, where an archetype classifies the resolution of pressures — §7 · 5. Archetypes

P

Policy and permission — design pressure: satisfy the rules that decide whether the project happens at all, and the subsidies that decide what pays; the one pressure whose direction the jurisdiction sets — §7 · 3.1 The pressures

Pore Space — the three-dimensional volume between modules and between modules and ground; frames clearance as habitat and working space — §7

R

Rangevoltaics — grazing under and between the panels (S2); the field’s ecological-literature name for solar grazing, workable with sheep but not with goats, cattle, or horses; syn. solar grazing — §7 · 1.2 Worked example: grazing

Regional calibration — a restatement of the lexicon’s indicative values for a particular region: capacity bands, absent zones, the binding constraint, the positions realistically open — §7 · 6.2 A worked calibration

Regional factors — the six spatial factors a region is read by: resource, grid operator, aridity, latitude, cropping system, irrigation — §7 · 6.1 Six factors

Regionality — the claim that the design pressures resolve differently in different places, because the factors that weight them vary across space; the vocabulary is constant, the weights are not — §7 · 6. Regionality

Restoration mode — whether restorative work is delivered by choice of ground, array geometry, or operation — Siting-led, Structure-led, or Management-led — §7 · 1.3 Siting latitude and the restoration mode shift

Restorative — the frame of the whole lexicon: ground-layer function as a design objective co-equal with generation — §7

Restorative goals — design pressure: make ecological, hydrological, and economic resilience co-equal with generation — §7 · 3.1 The pressures

Restorative function — a restorative capacity a project can be designed to deliver, reachable across many structural positions; a column of the archetype × function matrix — four biophysical (hydrologic, soil & carbon, biodiversity & habitat, microclimate) plus one socioeconomic (community & economic) — §7 · 4. Restorative Functions

S

S1 — Full sharing — production continues within the array footprint, in the same ground — §7 · 2.1 The positions

S2 — Functional sharing — ground layer productive but subordinate to the array; grazing, forage — §7 · 2.1 The positions

S3 — Coexistence — neither sharing nor sparing: the array takes ordinary ground outright and the revenue sustains the farm enterprise holding it, so the restoration is socioeconomic rather than biophysical; the center of the axis — §7 · 2.1 The positions

S4 — Idled-land sparing — array occupies ground already idle, marginal, or out of production, so productive land is spared; arrays on buildings take no ground and hold no position (A0) — §7 · 2.1 The positions

S5 — Impaired-land retirement — production ends on ground that carried a measurable off-site burden (low yields, high runoff, nutrient export, irrigation draw beyond the aquifer’s budget), or that sits where such a burden can be intercepted — §7 · 2.1 The positions

SCALE footprint — a whole-project reading across four domains (Sustainable Communities, Agriculture, Landscapes, Energy), each running from regeneration to depletion; carries direction, not a pass mark — §7 · The SCALE footprint

Scale Transect — a single gradient from farmstead to regional that most other terms are read against; defined by covarying bundles, not capacity — §7 · 1. The Scale Transect

Screening — management of the array’s outward visual presence (setbacks, buffers, berms, fencing, glint/glare); acts on the visual portion of the influence zone; dual-function — §7

Sharing–Sparing Axis — the scale-orthogonal dimension: whether the array shares land with production (land sharing), spares land by taking ground out of it (land sparing), or neither; five positions centered on S3 — §7 · 2. The Sharing–Sparing Axis

Siting latitude — the designer’s freedom over where the array goes; peaks at A2–A3, bounded by the parcel at A1 and by the land market and grid topology at A5 — §7 · 1.3 Siting latitude and the restoration mode shift

Siting-led — restoration mode: the restorative work is done by the choice of ground; requires A2–A3 latitude — §7 · 1.3 Siting latitude and the restoration mode shift

Community acceptance and benefit — design pressure: secure and sustain community acceptance and community benefit — two forces that can come apart, held in one pressure — §7 · 3.1 The pressures

Solar grazing — see Rangevoltaics — §7

Soil & carbon function — restorative function: perennial, low-disturbance cover rebuilding soil organic carbon and aggregate stability, against a construction-disturbance debt paid first — §7 · 4.2 Soil & carbon function

Stocking density — instantaneous animals per unit area at a moment; diverges from carrying capacity — §7 · 1.2 Worked example: grazing

Structure-led — restoration mode: array geometry is the instrument — clearance, row spacing, module transmissivity — §7 · 1.3 Siting latitude and the restoration mode shift

T

Tracking Schedule & Curtailment — operational-regime levers: how the array is steered through the day, and deliberate output reduction — §7

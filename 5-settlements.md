# 5. Settlements

The templates, restated as resolutions of competing pressures at a scale zone and sharing position. Following New Urbanism's Archetype → Prototype → Type → Stereotype progression, these are **Types**: real, transformable instances, not an exhaustive set.

**Overstory Agrivoltaic** *(vineyard, orchard)*
A2, occasionally A3 · **S1** · *structure-led*
*Dominant pressures:* agricultural continuity, social license.
*Yielded:* capital discipline (high clearance is expensive), generation yield (row spacing serves the crop).
*Signature levers:* high clearance, possibly semitransparent modules, row geometry set by crop light requirements.

**Grazed Habitat Array**
A4–A5 · **S2–S3** · *management-led*
*Dominant pressures:* generation yield, operational simplicity — grazing approaches cost parity with mowing rather than beating it outright, its vegetation-management advantage back-loaded to the mature-project years once the establishment invoices are behind, with restorative benefit following.
*Yielded:* agricultural continuity in the strong sense (grazing is a service contract, not the farm's own operation).
*Signature levers:* contract-enterprise management regime, rotation logistics, fence-gate topology, water access.

**Buffer-Integrated Array** *(prairie strips)*
A3 · **S3** · *siting-led*
*Dominant pressures:* restorative ambition with a specific service target (erosion, nutrient runoff), social license.
*Yielded:* some generation yield to buffer geometry.
*Signature levers:* placement logic along hydrologic features, ground-layer composition, conservation payment stacking.

**Marginal-Land Diversification Array**
A2–A3 · **S4** · *siting-led*
*Dominant pressures:* farm economic resilience, capital discipline.
*Yielded:* generation yield (marginal ground is rarely optimally shaped or oriented).
*Signature levers:* placement logic within field, ground layer determined by what marginal ground supports.

> **Note the kinship.** This settlement and the buffer-integrated one share an economic engine: prairie strips pay because they cost less than farming consistently low-yielding ground, which is the same partial-budget logic driving marginal-land solar siting. They differ mainly in whether the spared ground carries a designed ecological function or an energy function. Both are siting-led, and both depend on the A2–A3 latitude that makes choosing the ground possible at all — which is precisely why neither scales upward without changing into something else. Two settlements, one insight, and a good illustration of what the pressure framing reveals that a template catalog hides.

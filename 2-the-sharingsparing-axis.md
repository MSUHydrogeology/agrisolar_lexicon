# 2. The Sharing–Sparing Axis

### 2.1 The positions

The second dimension, orthogonal to [scale]({{ '/1-the-scale-transect/' | relative_url }}). Does the array **share** land with agricultural production, or **spare** land by occupying ground of low production value?

This borrows the land-sharing / land-sparing framing from conservation ecology, where the debate is authentically unsettled — which is precisely why it makes a good axis. It describes a real choice without ranking the options.

| Position | Description | Characteristic |
|---|---|---|
| **S1 — Full sharing** | Production continues within the array footprint, in the same ground | Overstory [agrivoltaic]({{ '/4-general-terminology/#agrivoltaics' | relative_url }}); crops between rows |
| **S2 — Functional sharing** | Ground layer is productive but subordinate to the array; grazing, forage | Solar grazing at any scale |
| **S3 — Substituted function** | Ground layer delivers ecological rather than agricultural function | Pollinator habitat, prairie/buffer |
| **S4 — Sparing** | Array occupies ground removed from production because it was low-yielding or already idle | Marginal-land siting, previously disturbed land |

**Why this axis and not another.** It is structural rather than an outcome; it is non-evaluative; and it separates the four [settlements]({{ '/5-settlements/' | relative_url }}) cleanly where scale alone does not. The [ecovoltaic]({{ '/4-general-terminology/#ecovoltaics' | relative_url }}) literature already reaches for land-sharing language, so this connects to an existing conversation rather than coining a private one.

### 2.2 The feasible region is a wedge, not a rectangle

The two dimensions are conceptually independent but not empirically free of one another, and the constraint runs through [siting latitude]({{ '/1-the-scale-transect/#14-siting-latitude-and-the-restoration-mode-shift' | relative_url }}) (§1.4).

**Sparing requires the freedom to choose the ground.** S4 is only available to a designer who can select which acres to occupy. At A4–A5, where siting is governed by contiguous land assembly near a substation, that freedom is largely gone — a five-hundred-acre block will contain productive and marginal ground indiscriminately. Large projects are therefore pushed toward S2–S3 by structural necessity rather than preference: if you cannot choose *which* ground, your only remaining option is to do something worthwhile with *all* of it.

![The feasible region is a wedge, not a rectangle]({{ '/figures/fig3_feasible_wedge.svg' | relative_url }})

***Figure 3.** The feasible region across both dimensions. All four sharing positions stay open through A3; the space narrows toward A5, where land assembly governs siting and sparing becomes largely unavailable.*

The practical readings:

- **S4 at A5 is close to unavailable.** Claims of marginal-land siting at utility scale deserve scrutiny about what fraction of the footprint is genuinely marginal.
- **S1 at A5 is rare for a different reason** — the clearance and row geometry that full sharing requires are hard to reconcile with utility-scale capital discipline.
- **The middle of the transect is the widest part of the space.** A3 is where all four sharing positions remain genuinely open, which is one reason mid-scale sites are disproportionately interesting as design exemplars.

**S1's appeal is not uniform across regions.** In-footprint crop benefit is strongly climate- and crop-conditional — pronounced in water-limited, high-radiation settings and for shade-tolerant crops (Barron-Gafford et al. 2019), but mixed to net-negative for staple grains and in temperate or humid settings where shade costs yield without a compensating water saving (Weselek et al. 2019). Full sharing is therefore not a uniformly attractive option across the transect.

This makes the framework mildly predictive rather than merely descriptive, and it is falsifiable — a documented S4 project at A5 would be a real counterexample worth examining.

**It also gives the definitional dispute a home.** American Farmland Trust's position — that [dual use]({{ '/4-general-terminology/#agrivoltaics' | relative_url }}) requires active agricultural production sustained across the project's 30–40 year life, within the array footprint — is structurally a mandate for S1–S2. Critics argue this marginalizes ecovoltaics by excluding pollinator habitat at S3. Putting both on a named axis lets the lexicon describe the disagreement precisely without adjudicating it. *(See §4, Agrivoltaics / Ecovoltaics.)*

### Associated Terminology

Terms this section introduces; cross-referenced terms link to their home section.

- **Sharing–Sparing Axis** — the second, scale-orthogonal dimension: whether the array shares land with production or spares low-value ground from it. Defined here.
- **S1 — Full sharing** — production continues within the array footprint, in the same ground.
- **S2 — Functional sharing** — ground layer is productive but subordinate to the array.
- **S3 — Substituted function** — ground layer delivers ecological rather than agricultural function.
- **S4 — Sparing** — array occupies ground removed from production because it was low-yielding or already idle.
- **Feasible wedge** — the feasible region across scale and sharing: all four positions stay open through A3, narrowing toward A5 where land assembly governs siting and sparing becomes largely unavailable. Defined here (§2.2).
- **Siting latitude** — the designer's freedom over where the array goes; the constraint that makes the region a wedge. See [§1.4]({{ '/1-the-scale-transect/#14-siting-latitude-and-the-restoration-mode-shift' | relative_url }}).
- **Agrivoltaics / Ecovoltaics** — production (S1–S2) versus ecological (S3) ground layers. See [§4]({{ '/4-general-terminology/#agrivoltaics' | relative_url }}).

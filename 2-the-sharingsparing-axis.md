# 2. The Sharing–Sparing Axis

### 2.1 The positions

The second dimension, orthogonal to scale. Does the array **share** land with agricultural production, or **spare** land by occupying ground of low production value?

This borrows the land-sharing / land-sparing framing from conservation ecology, where the debate is authentically unsettled — which is precisely why it makes a good axis. It describes a real choice without ranking the options.

| Position | Description | Characteristic |
|---|---|---|
| **S1 — Full sharing** | Production continues within the array footprint, in the same ground | Overstory agrivoltaic; crops between rows |
| **S2 — Functional sharing** | Ground layer is productive but subordinate to the array; grazing, forage | Solar grazing at any scale |
| **S3 — Substituted function** | Ground layer delivers ecological rather than agricultural function | Pollinator habitat, prairie/buffer |
| **S4 — Sparing** | Array occupies ground removed from production because it was low-yielding or already idle | Marginal-land siting, previously disturbed land |

**Why this axis and not another.** It is structural rather than an outcome; it is non-evaluative; and it separates the four settlements cleanly where scale alone does not. The ecovoltaic literature already reaches for land-sharing language, so this connects to an existing conversation rather than coining a private one.

### 2.2 The feasible region is a wedge, not a rectangle

The two dimensions are conceptually independent but not empirically free of one another, and the constraint runs through siting latitude (§1.4).

**Sparing requires the freedom to choose the ground.** S4 is only available to a designer who can select which acres to occupy. At A4–A5, where siting is governed by contiguous land assembly near a substation, that freedom is largely gone — a five-hundred-acre block will contain productive and marginal ground indiscriminately. Large projects are therefore pushed toward S2–S3 by structural necessity rather than preference: if you cannot choose *which* ground, your only remaining option is to do something worthwhile with *all* of it.

***Figure 3.** The feasible region across both dimensions. All four sharing positions stay open through A3; the space narrows toward A5, where land assembly governs siting and sparing becomes largely unavailable.*

The practical readings:

- **S4 at A5 is close to unavailable.** Claims of marginal-land siting at utility scale deserve scrutiny about what fraction of the footprint is genuinely marginal.
- **S1 at A5 is rare for a different reason** — the clearance and row geometry that full sharing requires are hard to reconcile with utility-scale capital discipline.
- **The middle of the transect is the widest part of the space.** A3 is where all four sharing positions remain genuinely open, which is one reason mid-scale sites are disproportionately interesting as design exemplars.

This makes the framework mildly predictive rather than merely descriptive, and it is falsifiable — a documented S4 project at A5 would be a real counterexample worth examining.

**It also gives the definitional dispute a home.** American Farmland Trust's position — that dual use requires active agricultural production sustained across the project's 30–40 year life, within the array footprint — is structurally a mandate for S1–S2. Critics argue this marginalizes ecovoltaics by excluding pollinator habitat at S3. Putting both on a named axis lets the lexicon describe the disagreement precisely without adjudicating it. *(See §4, Agrivoltaics / Ecovoltaics.)*
